## Research scope and non-approvals

This packet is **research and planning only**. It does **not** approve backtest implementation, connector work, data ingestion, external API calls, provider integrations, forecast pulls, runtime logic, automation, order placement, execution, autonomy, or position sizing.

The only approved output of this packet is a **validation methodology** for Weather Bot research. In MEG terms, the deliverable is a gating framework for the future PRD, not an engineering go-ahead. The right interpretation is: *what evidence must exist before implementation-adjacent work may even be proposed?* The answer throughout this report is intentionally conservative: if the required point-in-time evidence, source compatibility, or trap handling is missing, the correct posture is **fail_closed** or **defer**, not “implement and learn later.”

## Validation philosophy and target taxonomy

The validation philosophy should be source-compatible, event-time correct, calibration-first, trap-aware, and execution-realistic. WMO verification guidance emphasizes calibration, reliability, sharpness, and proper scoring rules for probabilistic forecasts, while time-series validation literature emphasizes rolling-origin evaluation and strict out-of-sample testing rather than shuffled cross-validation. Prediction-market literature, meanwhile, supports using market prices as informative signals but not treating them as frictionless or perfectly calibrated probabilities, and microstructure literature warns that executable returns differ materially from midpoint or mark-to-model returns. citeturn2search0turn2search2turn1search0turn21search9turn3search0turn3search1turn9search0turn9search13turn9search14

For Weather Bot, “ground truth” must mean **the same object the venue settles on**. That can be a final climate report, a preliminary climate report, a first-posted advisory, a best-track archive, a storm-events archive, a named station’s daily climatological summary, or another explicitly named source. Official NOAA and NHC documentation shows these objects can differ materially in timing, revision status, and authority, and Kalshi’s own rule materials explicitly separate source agency, expiration, close time, determination, settlement, and outcome-review discretion. citeturn18search14turn19search21turn8search2turn7search0turn7search2turn26search1turn26search2turn26search3turn27search0turn27search14

The required validation targets for MEG are therefore broader than “model skill.” They include canonical event identity extraction, venue-market mapping, source-resolution matching, provider/source compatibility, trap detection, threshold probability and uncertainty estimates, alpha claims, human-review notes, and no-trade/blocking decisions. That taxonomy follows directly from the prior packets and is also consistent with official verification guidance that stresses the importance of matching forecasts and observations appropriately before scoring them. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file2 citeturn24search12turn24search6turn24search7

### Canonical event mapping validation

Weather Bot should validate canonical mapping by creating a **gold-standard manually labeled sample** of markets across all relevant families and then testing whether the extraction pipeline reproduces the following fields exactly: market family; source product; station/location and station ID if applicable; geographic precision; time window; timezone/day definition; threshold/comparator; unit and rounding rule; trace treatment; missing-value treatment; fallback chain; revision/finality rule; event-classification authority; venue discretion/invalidation rule; and close, determination, and settlement timestamps. This exact-field approach follows directly from the prior packets and matches the observation/forecast-matching emphasis in verification guidance. fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file5 citeturn24search12turn26search1turn27search0

The canonical mapping test should use at least two metrics. The first is **fieldwise exact-match accuracy** on the critical contractual fields. The second is **blocking-ambiguity recall**, meaning the system’s ability to refuse unsafe market mappings where the wording leaves source, station, window, trace, or authority unresolved. A market with unresolved ambiguity should not be scored as “partially right”; it should be blocked or flagged for human review because WX-RESEARCH-03 already classified those conditions as severe traps. fileciteturn0file3

A mapping attempt counts as a **failure** if it misses any load-bearing field that could change settlement, if it silently defaults to convenience observables, if it cannot reconstruct the fallback chain, or if it assumes final data where the contract names preliminary or contemporaneous data. Those failure modes are exactly the mechanisms by which false edges arise in weather markets. fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file1

## Event-time control, leakage prevention, and source-compatible historical replay

The method baseline for Weather Bot should be **as-of historical replay**. In practice, that means every research row is reconstructed from the standpoint of the historical decision timestamp using only information that was actually published by then. General forecast-evaluation literature warns that leakage occurs when model inputs or labels include information unavailable at prediction time, and rolling-origin evaluation is the accepted remedy for single-series forecasting problems. citeturn20search0turn11search1turn3search0turn3search1

For Weather Bot, as-of replay has four separate clocks that must be preserved: the market clock, the forecast-publication clock, the observation-publication clock, and the resolver-finality clock. The first determines whether a trade was even possible; the second determines whether a forecast input was available; the third determines whether a nowcast or preliminary observation could have informed a decision; and the fourth determines which label object is legitimate for settlement. Official docs show these clocks often differ materially. GFS and GEFS run four times daily, HRRR updates hourly, LAMP updates hourly, NDFD updates around :25 and :55, NOWData mixes archived and preliminary data, Storm Events officialization lags by roughly 75–90 days after month end, and HURDAT2 is post-storm analysis using data not necessarily available in real time. citeturn4search0turn4search1turn4search2turn4search3turn5search0turn18search14turn8search2turn7search2

**Final-archive leakage control** should therefore be explicit. If a market resolves on first-posted or preliminary data, the historical label must come from that first-posted or preliminary layer. If that layer cannot be reconstructed, the relevant hypothesis cannot progress beyond documentation or manual case-study stage. Conversely, if a market explicitly resolves on a final official archive, the label should come from that final archive and not from earlier operational products. NOAA climate, severe, and tropical documentation all show why this distinction matters. citeturn18search14turn19search21turn8search2turn8search5turn7search0turn7search2

**Forecast-run leakage control** should use **public-availability time, not model initialization time**. A 12 UTC model cycle is not the same thing as a 12 UTC informational input if the product was disseminated later, or if a public endpoint only refreshed at a scheduled interval afterward. The same principle applies to ECMWF access paths, where real-time open data, rolling archives, and full archive access differ. If historical publication timestamps are unavailable, timing-alpha claims should not receive pass status. citeturn25search1turn25search15turn5search0turn4search3turn32search0turn32search2turn32search3

**Station/source selection leakage** must also be prevented. A researcher should not be allowed to choose whichever nearby station, grid point, or provider best explains the outcome after the fact. Station and source identity must be fixed ex ante by the parsed contract, then locked for replay. Station-history and siting metadata exist precisely because location and observing practice matter, and the forecast-verification literature separately warns about representativeness error when comparing point observations with gridded outputs. citeturn18search2turn18search5turn18search1turn24search6turn24search7turn24search12

### Trap-filter validation

WX-RESEARCH-03 should remain the controlling severity source. In practice, that means trap validation must measure both **false negatives** on blocking traps and **false positives** on caution traps, but the asymmetry should favor safety. Missing a blocking trap is more serious than over-warning on a caution trap because the former directly contaminates labels or falsely green-lights unsafe analysis. fileciteturn0file3

The trap filters should be validated on example markets with manual adjudication and grouped by the risk channels MEG cares about most: source mismatch, station ambiguity, window/day ambiguity, comparator/unit/rounding ambiguity, trace/missing ambiguity, preliminary-vs-final ambiguity, event-classification authority ambiguity, and venue fallback/invalidation/discretion ambiguity. Official weather archives show why each channel is distinct. NOWData is not the same as final official climate certification, Storm Events is delayed and officialized after preliminary reporting, HURDAT2 is post-storm analysis, and Kalshi may initiate outcome review or change source handling under specified conditions. citeturn18search14turn8search2turn8search5turn7search2turn26search2turn26search3

A trap-filter program counts as **failed** if blocking traps do not reliably suppress unsafe cases, if caution warnings provide no incremental reviewer value, or if “safe” results cluster in markets that later turn out to have source/station/finality ambiguity. Those are the signatures of a false-edge factory rather than a validation framework. fileciteturn0file3

### Simulation methodology and human-review validation

Simulation belongs at the **paper-simulation** level only. It should assume trading can occur only at executable bid/ask quotes, with depth constraints, fees, slippage, fill uncertainty, stale-quote risk, and adverse-selection exposure. Kalshi documents that quick orders execute immediately at the best available prices, limit orders can rest and, in some cases, partially execute, and trading fees apply to immediately matched orders under its fee schedule. Microstructure theory separately warns that observed prices and realizable returns diverge in the presence of spreads and information asymmetry. citeturn10search14turn10search3turn10search4turn10search0turn10search2turn9search14turn9search3

A research simulation should therefore report, at minimum, best bid/ask used, quote age, assumed fill rule, fee rule, spread paid, depth consumed, and whether the order was maker-like or taker-like. A market-edge claim should fail closed if it depends on midpoints, ignores partial fills/depth, assumes guaranteed fills at stale quotes, or omits fees. citeturn10search0turn10search2turn10search4turn9search14

Human-review validation should be treated as a first-class validation channel, not a UX afterthought. Because venue discretion, resolver fallback, and language ambiguity cannot always be resolved statistically, reviewers need structured notes showing the contract parse, source chain, finality layer, active traps, threshold distance, and data-availability caveats. The human-review layer should then be measured with inter-rater agreement and override auditing, using a statistic such as Cohen’s kappa for categorical block/caution/pass decisions and mandatory rationale capture for overrides. citeturn31search0turn26search2turn26search3

### Final handoff summary

The standalone Opus Weather Bot PRD should inherit four hard validation requirements from this packet. First, it must require **canonical event mapping and source-compatible labels** before any model comparison. Second, it must require **point-in-time replay with decision-time availability clocks**, explicitly preventing final-archive leakage and forecast-run leakage. Third, it must require **out-of-sample calibration evidence**, especially threshold-bucket calibration near strikes, before any “edge” language is permitted. Fourth, it must require **trap-filtered, executable-cost paper simulation plus human-review packets** before any implementation-adjacent ticket can be opened. fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file1 citeturn20search0turn1search0turn10search0turn10search4

The minimum evidence before any implementation-adjacent work is therefore: a gold mapping set; resolver-accurate historical labels; a documented as-of replay protocol; strict temporal and grouped OOS validation; calibration diagnostics that remain acceptable near thresholds; trap validation with WX-03 severities preserved; and a cost-aware paper simulation that uses executable quotes rather than midpoints. Anything less leaves MEG exposed to exactly the false-edge modes the earlier packets warned about. fileciteturn0file3 fileciteturn0file4 fileciteturn0file1

The biggest blockers are also now explicit: historical access to first-posted versus final resolver layers; history of full quote/depth data; provider publication timestamps rather than just nominal run cycles; and sufficient strictly compatible samples for severe/tropical families after trap filtering. Those blockers should appear in the PRD as gating dependencies, not as assumptions. citeturn18search14turn8search2turn32search3turn27search14

No implementation, ingestion, connectors, external API usage, runtime behavior, trading, order placement, position sizing, or autonomy is approved by this packet. This is the validation standard that future work would have to satisfy first.
